\subsection{Experiments 1 and 2: Correlation and Importance Analysis}

In Experiment 1, the correlation analysis workflow computed the Pearson correlation matrix over the features in the \texttt{PWDriverFeatureSet} dataset. In Experiment 2, we removed outliers from the \texttt{PWGPredictors} dataset using an isolation forest fitted on the predictors with a contamination rate of \(0.2\), then trained a random forest regressor on the filtered data. The resulting importance profile is conditional on this contamination setting and should be interpreted descriptively. Both experiments do not employ a train--test split.

\input{4_experiments/plots/correlation_matrix.tex}

Figure~\ref{fig:correlation_matrix} shows that GDP is strongly associated with plastic waste generation (\(r = 0.92\)). The correlation between urban population and plastic waste generation (\(r = 0.78\)) is stronger than that of total population (\(r = 0.71\)). Population and mismanaged plastic waste are also highly correlated (\(r = 0.94\)). In contrast, the percentage of plastic waste that is properly managed is nearly orthogonal to the scale variables and exhibits a weak negative correlation with plastic waste generation (\(r = -0.17\)).

Figure~\ref{fig:feature_importances} indicates that \textit{year} has the highest importance score (0.4355), followed by GDP (0.1576). The importance of urban population (0.1053) is slightly greater than rural population (0.0986) and total population (0.0631). This pattern is consistent with a recent India-specific study reporting urban--rural differences in plastic-waste mismanagement~\cite{sen2026unveiling}. The correlation and impurity-based feature-importance analyses are descriptive and predictive rather than causal; their values may reflect country scale, temporal trends, correlated predictors, and source composition.
