\subsection{Experiment 3: Reconstruction}

The AUDA analysis table is highly sparse due to substantial missing data across countries, regions, and years. For example, the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) reports plastic waste generation data only at five-year intervals between 2000 and 2015. Therefore, in Experiment 3, we reconstruct the missing values in the \texttt{YearTRC (Japan)} and \texttt{YearTRC (US)} datasets.

We randomly select 10\% of the observed data points in the dataset (with at least two points) as the test set and fit the models using the remaining 90\% of the data points. The test samples are guaranteed not to include the first or last data point in the time series, ensuring that the models are evaluated on their interpolation capabilities rather than extrapolation. Since the evaluation metrics are sensitive to the specific test samples selected by the random number generator, we repeat the experiment 16 times and report the average results.

Table~\ref{tab:reconstruction_japan_trc} shows that SVR performs best on the \texttt{YearTRC (Japan)} reconstruction task, achieving the lowest MAE, RMSE, WAPE, and MAPE among all evaluated methods. Its WAPE of 0.79\% is substantially lower than the next-best cubic spline interpolation result of 1.65\%. This suggests that, for this series, the nonlinear margin-based fit of SVR captures the local trajectory better than the interpolation baselines and the smoother regression models. However, all reported \(R^2\) values are negative, which reflects the instability of \(R^2\) when the test set is very small and the held-out values have limited variance. For this reason, we treat WAPE as the primary metric for interpreting reconstruction performance.

Table~\ref{tab:reconstruction_united_states_trc} shows a different pattern for the \texttt{YearTRC (US)} reconstruction task. GPR achieves the best overall performance, with the lowest MAE, RMSE, WAPE, and MAPE, and the highest \(R^2\). Linear interpolation is also competitive, which indicates that the United States resin-consumption series is smoother and easier to interpolate than the Japan polymer-consumption series. In contrast, SVR performs poorly on this dataset, showing that the model that works best on one sparse country-level series may not generalize to another.
