\subsection{Experiment 4: Hyperparameter-selection Sensitivity and Robustness}

In Experiment 4, we evaluate the sensitivity and robustness of the hyperparameter selection procedure to the standard-error tolerance coefficient \(c_{\mathrm{SE}}\) and the complexity ordering in Experiment 3 (United States). We perform a sweep over \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} \in \{0, 0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 1\}\) and compare the selected models to the best-performing models in terms of WAPE on the validation set. When \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0\), the procedure selects only the configuration with the best mean validation performance; when \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 1\), it reduces to the standard 1-SE rule. We record the number of candidate configurations that fall within the selection threshold, the percentage of selection units where the selected configuration changes, the WAPE of the best configuration, and the WAPE of the final selected configuration. The penalty is defined as the difference between the WAPE of the selected configuration and the WAPE of the best configuration. The complexity rank of a configuration is its position when all evaluated configurations are ordered from simplest to most complex. We record the complexity rank of the selected configuration.

We also evaluate the robustness of the complexity ordering by comparing the selected configurations when the signs of the complexity-ordering keys are flipped.

In Experiment 3, we repeatedly perform 16 imputation runs and record the mean of results. In this experiment, we evaluate the sensitivity and robustness of the hyperparameter selection on three tunable models: ridge regression, GPR, and SVR. Therefore, there are 48 selection units for this experiment.

The sweep results in Figure~\ref{fig:selection_sensitivity_to_se_tolerance} and Table~\ref{tab:selection_sensitivity} show that \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.1\) changes model selection in more than half of the selection units for each model while adding only a small WAPE penalty (\(0.01\%\) for ridge regression and GPR, and \(0.06\%\) for SVR); when \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.01\), the WAPE penalty for the three models is negligible (\(<0.01\%\)). Larger coefficients continue to simplify the selected configurations, but \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.5\) and \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 1\) lead to noticeably larger validation-performance penalties, particularly for SVR, where the WAPE penalty increases to 0.43\% and 1.10\%, respectively. In contrast, when \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0\), the procedure always selects the configuration with the best mean validation score, which may overfit to fold-level noise. When \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.01\), the number of candidate configurations for the three models ranges from 6.44 to 10.69.

Because we simply want to avoid cases where the selected configuration falls into a local optimum in the hyperparameter space, leading to overfitting, choosing \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.01\) allows the procedure to select the simplest configuration among several well-performing ones, preventing hyperparameter overfitting with minimal loss in validation performance. Therefore, with the grid search and random search setup used in this study, we adopt \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.01\).

The results in Table~\ref{tab:complexity_ordering_robustness} illustrate that flipping the signs of the complexity-ordering keys changed the selected configuration in 81.25--93.75\% of selection units. This confirms that the complexity ordering actively influences the selection. Nevertheless, the mean WAPE degradation is negligible (\(<0.01\%\)), suggesting that none of the configurations suffer from overfitting. Although we do not reproduce the exact overfitting configuration, we cannot rule out the possibility that the selected configuration corresponds to a local optimum in the hyperparameter space.
