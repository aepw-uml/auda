\subsection{Experiment 4: Hyperparameter-selection Sensitivity and Robustness}

In Experiment 4, we evaluate the sensitivity and robustness of the hyperparameter selection procedure to the standard-error tolerance coefficient \(c_{\mathrm{SE}}\) and the complexity ordering in Experiment 3 (United States). We perform a sweep over \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} \in \{0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1\}\) and compare the selected models to the best-performing models in terms of WAPE on the test set. When \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0\), the procedure selects only the configuration with the best mean validation performance; when \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 1\), it reduces to the standard one-standard-error rule. We also evaluate the robustness of the complexity ordering by comparing the selected models to the best-performing models in terms of WAPE on the test set.

In Experiment 3, we repeatedly perform 16 reconstruction runs, and in this experiment, we evaluate the sensitivity and robustness of the hyperparameter selection on three tunable models: ridge regression, GPR, and SVR. Therefore, there are 48 selection units for this experiment.

The sweep results in Figure~\ref{fig:selection_sensitivity_to_se_tolerance} and Table~\ref{tab:selection_sensitivity} show that \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.1\) changes model selection in more than half of the selection units for each model while adding only a small WAPE penalty (0.01\% for ridge regression and GPR, and 0.06\% for SVR). Larger coefficients continue to simplify the selected configurations, but \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.5\) and \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 1\) lead to noticeably larger validation-performance penalties, particularly for SVR where the WAPE penalty increases to 0.43\% and 1.10\%, respectively. In contrast, when \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0\), the procedure always selects the configuration with the best mean validation score, which may overfit to fold-level noise. Setting \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.1\) allows the procedure to choose the simplest configuration among several well-performing configurations, reducing hyperparameter overfitting with minimal loss in validation performance. Therefore, with the grid search and random search setup in this study, we use \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.1\).

The results in Table~\ref{tab:complexity_ordering_robustness} illustrate that flipping the signs of the complexity-ordering keys changed the selected configuration in 87.5--100\% of selection units. This confirms that the order is active in the selection procedure. After flipping the signs, the resulting WAPE differences were negligible, with mean degradation near zero and maximum degradation for SVR at 0.13 percentage points. This suggests that many configurations within the standard-error tolerance set are close in validation performance.
