	% Country-level plastic waste reporting is sparse, irregular, and heterogeneous: many nations record only a handful of observations across decades, and indicators differ in unit, cadence, and definitional scope. This data scarcity poses an analytic challenge: how should one forecast or reconstruct national-level trends when fewer than thirty observations are available, and when reporting frequency varies across countries and indicators? In this study, we present (i) PRISM, a large-language-model-assisted extract-transform-load pipeline that turns heterogeneous public sources into structured records, and AUDA, a schema-enforced analytical database derived from PRISM; and (ii) a comparative forecasting and reconstruction benchmark over country-level plastic waste indicators, including plastic waste generation, total resin consumption, and polymer PET consumption. We evaluate naive, statistical, and kernel- and neural-network-based machine learning methods, with hyperparameter selection guided by a one-standard-error rule applied over grid and random search. Across seven experiments spanning 6 to 695 samples, classical and statistical baselines remain competitive with, and on the smallest datasets frequently outperform, more flexible machine learning models, while multivariate neural-network forecasting yields gains when sufficient cross-country information is available. We use weighted absolute percentage error (WAPE) as the primary metric, and discuss the instability of the coefficient of determination on very small holdout sets. The pipeline and benchmark together provide a reproducible foundation for future modeling and policy analysis on plastic waste data.
	Plastic waste planning depends on country-level data that are often sparse, irregular, and heterogeneous across sources. This study presents AUDA, an analytical workflow built from validated records in the PRISM data ecosystem, for constructing modeling datasets and benchmarking reconstruction and forecasting methods under these realistic data constraints. The AUDA analytical database contains more than 28{,}000 verified records across 195 countries and regions, covering demographic, socioeconomic, waste-management, resin-consumption, and polymer-specific indicators. Using these records, we evaluate correlation and feature-importance analysis, missing-value reconstruction, univariate temporal forecasting, pooled neural-network forecasting, and country-specific multivariate forecasting. The benchmark compares interpolation and time-series baselines, statistical models, ridge regression, Gaussian process regression, support vector regression, random forests, and multilayer perceptrons. We also introduce a selection-efficiency coefficient for the one-standard-error hyperparameter-selection rule, allowing the tolerance for validation-error uncertainty to be adjusted when selecting simpler configurations. Across the experiments, no single model is uniformly best. Support vector regression performs strongly in several reconstruction and global forecasting settings, simple statistical baselines remain competitive for short country-level time series, and neural-network forecasting benefits from pooled pretraining in some multivariate settings but remains sensitive to data heterogeneity and small holdout sets. Weighted absolute percentage error is used as the primary metric because target magnitudes vary widely across countries and indicators, while \(R^2\) is interpreted cautiously for very small test sets. Overall, AUDA provides a reproducible foundation for evaluating plastic-waste models and shows that reliable forecasting in this domain requires matching model complexity and validation strategy to the amount and quality of available data.
