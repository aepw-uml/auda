\section{Conclusion}

This study presents AUDA, a workflow for constructing analytical tables from human-reviewed records extracted by PRISM and conducting descriptive and predictive analyses of plastic-related data.
The case studies illustrate correlation analysis, random-forest feature importance, interpolation, and temporal forecasting using sparse country-level records and an external global production series.
AUDA supports these analyses but does not reconcile differences in definitions, data collection procedures, policy effects, or statistical methods across source organizations.

In the two interpolation case studies, cubic spline interpolation achieved the lowest mean WAPE for Japan, while GPR achieved the lowest mean WAPE for the United States.
Under rolling-origin forecasting, Theil--Sen regression achieved the lowest mean WAPE for global plastic production, while persistence and ARIMA tied for the lowest mean WAPE for Japanese plastic waste generation.
However, the small number of series and observations limits the extent to which these rankings can be generalized.
Likewise, the descriptive analyses do not establish causal relationships or estimate the effects of policy interventions.

We also explored pooled prediction of plastic waste generation using a multilayer perceptron (MLP)~\cite{goodfellow2016deep}, which yielded a mean WAPE of \(75.21\%\) across 16 random holdout runs.
However, this exploratory experiment lacked comparisons with baseline models and evaluation on unseen countries or future years, limiting the conclusions that could be drawn.
We therefore omit a detailed presentation of this exploratory experiment.

The standard-error tolerance coefficient makes the validation-error threshold used in hyperparameter selection adjustable.
The sensitivity analysis shows that both this coefficient and the chosen complexity ordering can affect which configuration is selected.
Because the analysis uses a single series with nine observations and compares validation errors, it does not establish improved performance on unseen data or identify a generally preferable coefficient.

Reproducibility is limited to downstream analyses using the frozen datasets and code.
External researchers cannot independently repeat the restricted upstream extraction and validation process.

The experimental results show that traditional machine learning models, such as ridge regression and SVR, do not perform noticeably better than baseline and statistical models in the interpolation and forecasting case studies.
Future work will investigate models that distinguish variation in plastic-related indicators from differences associated with source organizations, including their definitions, data collection procedures, and statistical methods.
One possible approach is to jointly model underlying environmental patterns and source-specific variation, drawing inspiration from variational autoencoders (VAEs) used to account for technical variation in single-cell data~\cite{he2024mosaic}.
This approach would require preserving source identifiers and examining whether observations from the same organization share common reporting characteristics.
Further evaluation would be needed to determine whether such models can separate source effects from genuine environmental differences and improve forecasting or imputation.
