The experimental results do not point to one model that works best in every situation. Support Vector Regression (SVR) performs well in global plastic production forecasting, which is a single-source time-series dataset with over 50 samples; it also performs relatively well in both imputation tasks. However, simple interpolation, smoothing, and statistical baselines remain competitive for short country-level series, where machine learning models can easily overfit or become unstable. The pooled neural-network experiment is an exploratory benchmark: its performance is limited by the heterogeneity of the underlying records and does not support deployment as a prediction model. Since most country-level time-series datasets used in this study are short and noisy, the experimental results are provided for reference only.

The proposed standard-error tolerance coefficient for the one standard error (1-SE) rule provides a practical way to examine validation performance alongside model simplicity. In the imputation sensitivity study, when \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.01\), the selected hyperparameter configuration often differs from the minimum mean validation-error configuration, while the reported average WAPE penalty is 0.00\%. Larger coefficients expand the candidate range and select simpler configurations, but \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.5\) and \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 1\) produce larger validation-performance penalties. Overall, the results show that the standard-error tolerance coefficient provides an empirical trade-off between validation error and model simplicity in this benchmark.

Reports from different organizations may use different approaches to collecting and processing data, which can lead to inconsistencies. Future work should take into account the metadata associated with each migrated data point, such as the publication details and the organization that produced the source report, and propose an innovative and effective architecture for measuring the relationship between data and their corresponding metadata.
