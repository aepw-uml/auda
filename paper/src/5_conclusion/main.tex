\section{Conclusion}

This study leverages PRISM, a data platform developed by AEPW that extracts plastic waste-related records from heterogeneous sources using large language models and other tools. It also presents AUDA, an analytical tool that migrates human-reviewed extracted records from PRISM into structured analytical tables and produces reproducible modeling workflows.

We used these workflows to evaluate correlation and feature importance, missing-value imputation, univariate temporal forecasting, and pooled neural-network prediction. The experimental results do not point to one model that works best in every situation. Support Vector Regression (SVR) performs well in global plastic production forecasting, which is a single-source time-series dataset with over 50 samples; it also performs relatively well in both imputation tasks. However, simple interpolation, smoothing, and statistical baselines remain competitive for short country-level series, where machine learning models can easily overfit or become unstable. The pooled neural-network experiment is an exploratory case study: its performance is limited by the heterogeneity of the underlying records and does not support deployment as a prediction model. Since most country-level time-series datasets used in this study are short and noisy, the experimental results are provided for reference only.

The proposed standard-error tolerance coefficient for the one standard error (1-SE) rule provides a practical way to examine validation performance alongside model simplicity. In the imputation sensitivity study, when \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.01\), the selected hyperparameter configuration often differs from the minimum mean validation-error configuration, while the reported average WAPE penalty is 0.00\%. Larger coefficients expand the candidate range and select simpler configurations, but \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.5\) and \(c_{\mathrm{SE}} = 1\) produce larger validation-performance penalties. Overall, the results show that the standard-error tolerance coefficient provides an empirical trade-off between validation error and model simplicity in this benchmark.

Future work will evaluate provenance-aware hierarchical models that incorporate source organization, publication date, measurement definition, unit-conversion history, and other record-level metadata when estimating plastic-waste outcomes.

Reports from different organizations often rely on different data collection and processing protocols, resulting in systematic inconsistencies across sources. Analyzing such noisy, heterogeneous data therefore requires accounting for the provenance of each observation, for example by incorporating the reporting organization as a predictor. Future work will develop a model that jointly learns latent environmental states and source-specific technical variation from the heterogeneous dataset, under the assumption that observations from the same organization share a common technical-noise distribution. Such variation may arise from differences in organizational protocols, measurement-instrument error, preprocessing procedures, and other source-specific factors. In bioinformatics, variational autoencoders (VAEs) have recently been used to model and disentangle technical variation associated with different laboratories and experimental batches~\cite{he2024mosaic}. By identifying and removing organization-specific technical variation, we may recover cleaner estimates of the underlying environmental states and thereby improve the accuracy of forecasting and imputation.
