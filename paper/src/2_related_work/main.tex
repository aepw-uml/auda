\section{Related Work}

Environmental forecasting is often limited by sparse observations, heterogeneous reporting practices, and noisy measurements. Large comparative studies show that regression-based methods can be competitive with, and sometimes outperform, classical time-series methods in environmental forecasting when overfitting is controlled~\cite{effrosynidis2023time}. In waste-management applications, machine learning methods such as support vector regression and neural networks have been used to forecast municipal solid waste generation from socioeconomic and demographic predictors~\cite{abbasi2013forecasting}. These studies motivate the use of statistical and machine learning models for environmental forecasting, but they often focus on a few datasets or a single modeling task.

Plastic-waste analysis comes with an extra challenge because country-level records are irregular across time and uneven across indicators. Prior global studies have projected plastic production, plastic waste generation, and mismanaged waste under different policy or consumption scenarios~\cite{lebreton2019future,dokl2024global,pottinger2024pathways}. These works provide important large-scale estimates, but they do not directly address the reproducible benchmarking problem studied in this paper, which explores how different statistical and machine learning models behave when applied to sparse country-level analytical tables derived from heterogeneous source records.

A related area of work focuses on model selection. Hyperparameter tuning is commonly performed using grid search, random search, and cross-validation~\cite{bergstra2012random,kohavi1995study,hyndman2018forecasting}. However, selecting the configuration with the lowest validation error can overfit the model-selection procedure itself, especially when validation estimates have high variance~\cite{cawley2010over}. The one-standard-error rule addresses this issue by preferring simpler configurations whose validation error is close to the best observed error~\cite{breiman2017classification,hastie2009elements}.
