\section{Related Work}

Sparse observations, heterogeneous reporting practices, and noisy measurements often constrain environmental forecasting. Large comparative studies show that regression-based methods can be competitive with, and sometimes outperform, classical time-series methods in environmental forecasting when overfitting is controlled~\cite{effrosynidis2023time}. In waste management applications, machine learning methods such as support vector regression and neural networks have been used to forecast municipal solid waste generation using socioeconomic and demographic predictors~\cite{abbasi2013forecasting}. These studies motivate the use of statistical and machine learning models for environmental forecasting, but they often focus on a few datasets or a single modeling task.

Plastic waste analysis can be challenging due to the data not being completely standardized across time and organizations. Prior studies have projected plastic production, plastic waste generation, and mismanaged waste under different policy or consumption scenarios~\cite{lebreton2019future,dokl2024global,pottinger2024pathways}. These works provide significant large-scale estimates, but they do not apply different statistical and machine learning models, especially neural network models, to sparse country-level analytical tables derived from heterogeneous source records.

A related area of work focuses on model selection. Hyperparameter tuning is typically performed using methods such as grid search, random search, and cross-validation~\cite{bergstra2012random,kohavi1995study,hyndman2018forecasting}. However, selecting the configuration yielding the lowest validation error on every occasion may result in choosing an overly complex configuration that causes overfitting. This phenomenon is particularly pronounced when the validation error estimations have high variance~\cite{cawley2010over}. The one standard error rule addresses this issue by selecting the simplest configuration whose validation error is within one standard error of the best observed error~\cite{breiman2017classification,hastie2009elements}.
