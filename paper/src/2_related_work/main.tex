\section{Related Work}

Sparse observations, heterogeneous reporting practices, and noisy measurements often constrain environmental forecasting. Large comparative studies show that regression-based methods can be competitive with, and sometimes outperform, classical time-series methods in environmental forecasting when overfitting is controlled~\cite{effrosynidis2023time}. In waste management applications, machine learning methods such as support vector regression and neural networks have been used to forecast municipal solid waste generation using socioeconomic and demographic predictors~\cite{abbasi2013forecasting}. These studies motivate the use of statistical and machine learning models for environmental forecasting, but they often focus on a few datasets or a single modeling task.

Research on the quantitative modeling of plastic waste has primarily developed along two paths. The first path involves material flow inventory analysis and scenario forecasting.
Geyer et al. provided the foundational global data of plastic production, use, and end-of-life fates~\cite{geyer2017production}, while subsequent studies projected future waste generation and mismanaged plastic volumes under alternative policy and consumption scenarios~\cite{lebreton2019future,borrelle2020predicted,lau2020evaluating,dokl2024global,pottinger2024pathways}.
Although these studies yielded influential large-scale estimates, they were not designed to compare the predictive accuracy of statistical models to machine learning models against observational records.
The second path applies machine learning to waste forecasting by training support vector regression, tree ensemble models, and neural networks on socioeconomic and demographic indicators such as population and GDP.
More closely aligned with the context of the present study, existing research has utilized neural networks to forecast plastic waste generation and evaluate the efficacy of interventions~\cite{fan2022forecasting}.
AUDA differs from both of these paths: it offers a reproducible multitask benchmarking framework that compares the performance of interpolation methods, statistical models, and machine learning models using sparse country-level analysis tables, with a specific emphasis on aligning model complexity and validation strategies with available data.

This study builds directly upon the PRISM data ecosystem, which is developed through a collaboration between the Alliance to End Plastic Waste and IBM.
The PRISM platform integrates heterogeneous data sources and converts them into standardized plastic waste information.
To date, no peer-reviewed research has previously utilized records derived by PRISM as a foundation for systematic model benchmarking.
To our knowledge, AUDA is among the first analytical workflows to transform human-reviewed extracted PRISM records into standardized "country-year" analysis tables and to use these data to establish reproducible benchmarks for imputation and forecasting.

Research in related fields also addresses the issue of model selection.
Hyperparameter tuning typically employs methods such as grid search, random search, and cross-validation~\cite{bergstra2012random,kohavi1995study,hyndman2018forecasting}.
However, consistently selecting the configuration with the lowest validation error may lead to the choice of an overly complex configuration, resulting in possible overfitting.
This phenomenon is particularly pronounced when the validation error estimate has high variance~\cite{cawley2010over}.
The one standard error (1-SE) rule mitigates this issue by selecting the simplest configuration whose validation error is within one standard error of the best observed error~\cite{breiman2017classification,hastie2009elements}.
