\section{Introduction}

Plastic waste has become one of the most critical environmental challenges of the twenty-first century, with global waste volumes expected to increase substantially in the coming decades.
Recent studies estimate that global plastic production exceeded 430 million tonnes (Mt) in 2022~\cite{houssini2025complexities} and will increase to approximately 735.4--832.4~Mt by 2050~\cite{dokl2024global}.
Correspondingly, the OECD projects that annual plastic waste generation will increase from 353~Mt in 2019 to 1{,}014~Mt by 2060~\cite{oecd2022PlasticWaste2060}.
The mismanagement of plastic waste further exacerbates environmental degradation.
While between 60 and 99~Mt of mismanaged plastic waste entered the environment in 2015, one report projected that this amount could nearly triple by 2060~\cite{lebreton2019future}.
These trends highlight the urgent need for robust, data-driven forecasting tools to support proactive planning and evidence-based intervention strategies.

In response to these challenges, recent research underscores the pivotal role of policy interventions in mitigating future plastic waste mismanagement.
Using a comprehensive global systems model, Pottinger et al.~\cite{pottinger2024pathways} demonstrate that a wide range of policy measures can substantially reduce mismanaged plastic waste by 2050.
These measures include minimum recycled-content mandates, improved recycling collection rates, packaging reuse requirements, consumption taxes, and targeted investments in waste management and recycling infrastructure.
Designing and implementing effective interventions requires data-driven insights into the temporal dynamics of plastic waste generation.
However, current decision-making frameworks used by governments, non-governmental organizations, and industry often rely on incomplete datasets and static estimates, limiting their ability to support forward-looking planning and effective intervention.
To help address these gaps, the Alliance to End Plastic Waste (AEPW) has invested in the Plastics Recovery Insights Steering Model (PRISM) data ecosystem~\cite{edie2023IBMPrismPlatform}.
This ecosystem leverages large language models (LLMs) to extract diverse data points from unstructured sources, including government reports, academic publications, and industry datasets, thereby creating a comprehensive repository of plastic waste-related information.

Nevertheless, applying analytical models to these records remains challenging because the observations are heterogeneous and often form short or incomplete time series.
This limits the evidence available for comparing interpolation and forecasting methods and makes model selection results difficult to interpret.
This study examines these practical limitations through selected case studies.

This paper presents AUDA (AEPW x UMass Lowell Data Analysis), a data modeling tool for analyzing data migrated from PRISM.
It aims to document this analytical process and illustrate its use through selected case studies on plastic production, waste generation, and resin consumption.
AUDA transforms human-reviewed PRISM records into a country-year analytical database and provides reusable workflows for constructing modeling datasets, filtering anomalous observations, imputing missing values, forecasting temporal trends, and evaluating model performance.
Using AUDA, we conduct a series of experiments on plastic waste and related driver variables, including demographic, socioeconomic, waste-management, resin-consumption, and polymer-specific indicators.
In addition, this paper serves as a preliminary study examining the use of an analytical workflow on selected sparse and heterogeneous plastic-related datasets.
The case studies presented in this paper examine only descriptive relationships and predictive performance; they do not estimate the effects of policy interventions or establish causal relationships.

This paper also introduces a variant of the 1-SE rule for hyperparameter selection.
The rule selects the simplest configuration whose mean validation error falls within a specified standard-error threshold of the minimum mean validation error.
We introduce a standard-error tolerance coefficient, \(c_{\mathrm{SE}}\), to adjust this threshold and examine how the resulting candidate set affects hyperparameter selection.
We treat this coefficient as an exploratory sensitivity setting rather than a correction to the standard-error estimate.

In brief, this paper makes two main contributions.
First, we present AUDA and use it to construct and analyze country-year datasets from human-reviewed PRISM records.
We illustrate its use through selected case studies covering descriptive analysis, missing-value interpolation, and temporal forecasting of plastic-related indicators.
Second, we explore how a standard-error tolerance coefficient and alternative complexity orderings affect hyperparameter selection in an interpolation case study.
